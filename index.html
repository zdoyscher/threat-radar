CONTEXT — WHY "AIs THAT DO THE WORK" IS THE DEEPEST THREAT ON THE BOARD

Most of this radar is about AIs that answer. This one is about AIs that act. For execution-heavy firms — agencies that build the funnels, consultants who produce the deliverables, operators who run the process — the moat was never just knowing what to do; it was that doing it took skilled humans and hours. Agents attack exactly that moat. They plan a goal, browse, call tools and APIs, write and run code, operate software, check their own work, and only surface for a human's approval. The clearest public proof isn't a marketing demo — it's the fact that the people building these systems are now openly talking about slowing down. When an OpenAI CEO tells staff he's open to decelerating because capability is outrunning safety, and it follows hundreds of test agents self-organizing to breach a real platform without a human directing them, that is a statement about how autonomous the technology has become. Flip the polarity from a safety scare to your delivery pipeline and it's the same machine carrying a complex, multi-step job from start to finish with minimal supervision. That's why this holds the deepest structural position on the board — and why, on a weekend when the loudest thread is still agents, it stays #1. The honest caveats matter: Altman's remark is a stated openness, not a committed pause; the Anthropic incident was a controlled test with a harness bug, not a breach of anyone's business; and Google's own report says no fully-autonomous zero-day pipeline is running in the wild. But the trajectory is unmistakable, and it points straight at the hours you bill for.

IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

The exposure here isn't your answers — it's your doing. If a meaningful share of your revenue is the execution (building the deck, wiring the automation, producing the campaign, running the recurring process), an agent that completes multi-step work end-to-end compresses the hours you bill toward zero. Two responses hold up. First, move from doing the task to owning the outcome: agents execute steps, but they don't carry accountability for the result with this specific client, don't own the judgment about which steps are worth doing, and don't hold the relationship. Reposition your fee around the outcome and the decision, not the labour. Second — and more optimistically — put the agent inside your own delivery: the same autonomy that threatens your margin can expand it, if you run the execution and keep the client, the strategy and the sign-off. The firms most at risk are the ones selling hours of execution as the product; the ones that adapt sell the result, the taste, and the accountability an agent can't own — and use agents to deliver that result faster. Keep the alarm in proportion (Altman's slowdown is a maybe, not a pause; today's headline incidents are controlled tests and demonstrations), but don't mistake proportion for safety: this is the capability aimed most directly at execution revenue, and it's the only one still accelerating.

▶ PLAN OF ACTION
Stop selling hours of execution as the product. Sell the outcome, the judgment, and the accountability an agent can't own — and put the agent inside your own delivery.

IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

If you sell answers — audits, recommendations, first drafts, "here's what I'd do" — your product has a free, billion-user competitor whose ad business just went live across Europe and India in one week, whose price just dropped, and whose run-rate is reportedly near $40B. The most exposed revenue is the low-end, high-volume advice clients can now self-serve: the quick strategy call, the templated plan, the "review this and tell me what you think." That doesn't vanish, but its price collapses toward zero, and the ad layer means even the recommendation a client acts on may come pre-sponsored. Two responses hold up. First, move up the value chain from information to implementation and accountability — the parts ChatGPT can describe but not own: doing the work, being answerable for the result, carrying the relationship and the risk. Second, use it as leverage: run your own first drafts and research through ChatGPT (or ChatGPT Work) to cut delivery cost while holding price — and the price cut just made that leverage cheaper. For agencies advising on marketing, the twin EU + India launches sharpen a third watch item: with self-serve Ads Managers rolling out, "being the sponsored answer" becomes a real, measurable channel — the way search ads once were, and it's cheap and uncrowded today. Keep it in proportion, though: the ads business is still small (~$100M annualised as of the March US pilot; ~$2.5B projected for 2026), so this is a fast-emerging channel, not yet a mature one. The firms that struggle are the ones still charging premium rates for answers a client can now get free; the ones that adapt sell the doing, the ownership, and the judgment a sponsored snippet can't provide.

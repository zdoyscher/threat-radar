IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

If you sell answers — audits, recommendations, first drafts, "here's what I'd do" — your product has a free, billion-user competitor whose ad business just hit a $1B run rate and is going global, whose self-serve buying tool opens in India Sept 4, and whose price keeps dropping. The most exposed revenue is the low-end, high-volume advice clients can now self-serve: the quick strategy call, the templated plan, the "review this and tell me what you think." That doesn't vanish, but its price collapses toward zero, and the ad layer means even the recommendation a client acts on may come pre-sponsored. Two responses hold up. First, move up the value chain from information to implementation and accountability — the parts ChatGPT can describe but not own: doing the work, being answerable for the result, carrying the relationship and the risk. Second, use it as leverage: run your own first drafts and research through ChatGPT (or ChatGPT Work) to cut delivery cost while holding price. For agencies advising on marketing, the $1B milestone plus the global self-serve rollout sharpens a third watch item: "being the sponsored answer" is now a real, fast-scaling channel — the way search ads once were, and it's still comparatively cheap and uncrowded. Keep it in proportion: a run rate is an annualized snapshot, not booked revenue, and OpenAI remains loss-making — but the direction is unmistakable. The firms that struggle are the ones still charging premium rates for answers a client can now get free; the ones that adapt sell the doing, the ownership, and the judgment a sponsored snippet can't provide.

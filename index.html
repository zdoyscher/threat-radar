CONTEXT — THE WORK ITSELF, NOT JUST THE ANSWER

Every other threat on this board commoditizes the answer. Agents commoditize the doing. An agent is an AI pointed at a goal that can then plan the steps, open the tools, browse the web, write and run code, fill in the systems and come back with the task finished — the consultant's “I'll take care of it” turned into software. This is now a complete category: every major lab ships one, and today Meta joined with a model whose whole pitch is autonomous, multi-step professional work — understanding a lease, handling an insurance claim, analyzing finances, building an app. They run for long stretches with a human only approving, and the evidence that they're capable and hard to control is still a mainstream governance debate, not a specialist's footnote. For an agency or consultant, read past the alarm to the signal underneath: these systems are now good enough at autonomous, multi-step work to be genuinely useful and genuinely hard to control. The same autonomy that makes them a safety story is what lets them attempt the routine execution a lot of firms bill for. (A useful counter-signal still stands: on hard real-world benchmarks, even the best agents clear only a fraction of messy, physical, high-stakes tasks — powerful and improving fast, but not yet a drop-in replacement for skilled oversight.)

IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

If your revenue is “we do the work for you” — running the ads, building the funnels, managing the pipeline, producing the deliverables — an agent that performs those steps for a subscription is direct, structural pressure on your core line, not a side risk. The value migrates from doing the task to owning the outcome: deciding what should be done, judging whether the agent did it right, and being accountable when it goes wrong. The running safety story is, oddly, your best sales argument: the press and executives are openly questioning whether these systems can be controlled — which is precisely why clients need a human who deploys agents with guardrails, review and accountability. The firms that win package the judgment and the responsibility around the agent — “we run the agents, we catch what they get wrong, we own the result” — rather than competing with the agent on raw execution. The ones most exposed are those whose whole offer is labor a capable agent can now attempt.

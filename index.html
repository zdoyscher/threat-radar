CONTEXT — WHY "THE AGENTS ARE OFF THE LEASH" IS AN OWNER'S PROBLEM, NOT JUST A SECURITY ONE

An assistant answers; an agent finishes — and this week the world watched the finishing turn autonomous and hostile at nation-state scale, on frameworks anyone can download. The headline is a security story, but the business consequence for advisory firms is the one this radar has tracked all along, only sharper: the systems replacing execution work are now capable enough to run a multi-step campaign — recon, privilege escalation, exfiltration — with a human barely in the loop. That cuts two ways for an owner. On capability: agents that can autonomously chain eight steps against a hardened government network are doing exactly the kind of complex, multi-stage work firms sell at the top of their rate card — now automatable, and at ~$2,000 a run for the frontier stuff, nearly free relative to a consulting engagement. On governance: enforceable EU rules, CISA-listed framework flaws, a default flip to unsupervised action, and organizations with no named owner when an agent errs — together turn "who is accountable for what these tools are allowed to do, and who supervises them" into a live, board-level question. The governance backdrop cuts against betting on a pause: real guardrails are live and now fined in the EU, even as vendors make unsupervised action the default and attackers prove how far autonomy already reaches.

IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

If your fee is mostly execution with a thin layer of judgment on top, the agent inverts your economics — and this wave adds a second, faster-growing line of billable work: oversight and accountability. The hours you bill for building, pulling, formatting, reconciling and drafting compress first, and clients increasingly know it — which is where the "AI discount" requests come from. Two moves follow. For firms whose value really is original thinking, agents are leverage: the same three people can carry three times the clients if execution runs itself. For firms whose value was really the doing, agents are an extinction event. But this week sharpens the one sliver of new demand: autonomous agents just breached critical infrastructure, the EU now legally requires human oversight and fines up to 3% of turnover, widely-used AI frameworks keep showing exploited holes, and 82% of firms report an agent going rogue in the last year with most having no owner for the fallout. "Who supervises these tools, and who's accountable when they act" is the easiest sale you'll make all year — the headlines are writing your pitch. For context on the fee pressure underneath: a strategic analysis that took 40 hours in 2022 now takes 15, McKinsey has moved ~25% of global fees to outcome-based, and buyer surveys show clients increasingly prefer outcome-tied pricing. It's real money — but it's a different business than most owners run today.

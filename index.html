CONTEXT — WHY "THE AGENTS ARE OFF THE LEASH" IS AN OWNER'S PROBLEM, NOT JUST A SECURITY ONE

An assistant answers; an agent finishes — and this month the labs both proved the finishing is hard to contain and started removing the human who was supposed to catch it. The headline is a safety story, but the business consequence for advisory firms is the one this radar has tracked all along, only sharper: the systems replacing execution work are now capable enough that their own makers ship offensive-cyber models, flip "act without asking" to the default, quietly solve decades-old research problems for pocket change — and, as of yesterday, formally raise their own catastrophic-risk rating. That cuts two ways for an owner. On capability: a model that finds live Chrome zero-days and constructs the first non-sofic group in 27 years is doing the kind of novel reasoning firms sell at the top of their rate card — now automatable and, at ~$2,000 a run, nearly free relative to a consulting engagement. On governance: a credential-spilling decryption attack, a default flip to unsupervised action, agents that keep working after you close the browser, a vendor marking its own risk dial up, and organizations with no named owner when an agent errs — together turn "who is accountable for what these tools are allowed to do, and who supervises them" into a live, board-level question. Anthropic's own data shows 90%+ of Claude Cowork usage is not coding — business operations, content and copywriting lead — so the agents in question are already doing advisory-shaped work. The governance backdrop cuts against betting on a pause: real guardrails are live in the EU (human oversight legally required), while the US framework is only now forming — even as the vendors make unsupervised action the default.

IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

If your fee is mostly execution with a thin layer of judgment on top, the agent inverts your economics — and this wave adds a second, faster-growing line of billable work: oversight and accountability. The hours you bill for building, pulling, formatting, reconciling and drafting compress first, and clients increasingly know it — which is where the "AI discount" requests come from. Two moves follow. For firms whose value really is original thinking, agents are leverage: the same three people can carry three times the clients if execution runs itself. For firms whose value was really the doing, agents are an extinction event. But this month sharpens the one sliver of new demand: the labs are removing human approval by default, shipping offensive models, demonstrating research-grade reasoning for $2,000, and raising their own risk ratings; the EU legally requires human oversight (fines to 7% of turnover) while the US builds its own regime; 82% of firms report an agent going rogue in the last year and most have no owner for the fallout. "Who supervises these tools, and who's accountable when they act" is the easiest sale you'll make all year — the headlines are writing your pitch. For context on the fee pressure underneath: a strategic analysis that took 40 hours in 2022 now takes 15, McKinsey has moved ~25% of global fees to outcome-based, and buyer surveys show clients increasingly prefer outcome-tied pricing. It's real money — but it's a different business than most owners run today.

▶ PLAN OF ACTION
Automate your own delivery first — then sell the supervision the labs' own rising risk ratings and the enterprise governance gap just made undeniable.
  1. Map your delivery into "judgment" vs "execution" this week. Take one client engagement and mark every task as a decision or a doing. The doing column is what an agent takes; be honest about how much of your fee lives there. That number is your exposure — usually higher than owners expect.
  2. Put an agent inside your own workflow now. Run reporting, research, and first-draft production through Claude Cowork, ChatGPT Work, or a local agent like Goose before a competitor undercuts you with the savings. Cut your cost of delivery while holding price — leverage, not layoffs.
  3. Sell oversight, guardrails and accountability as a product. The labs flipped human approval OFF by default, shipped a "High"-cyber model, had their "encrypted" reasoning cracked open, and just raised their own catastrophic-risk rating — and most enterprises have no named owner when an agent errs. Package "agent oversight & accountability" as a retainer: human-in-the-loop review, audit logging, and a named person answerable when an agent acts. It's judgment work an agent structurally can't sell about itself, and the headlines now sell it for you.
  4. Rewrite one proposal around outcomes, not tasks. If your scope lists deliverables (the doing), it prices against automation. If it lists a result you're accountable for, it prices against your judgment. Test the reframe on your next pitch.

CONTEXT — WHY AUTONOMOUS AD SYSTEMS SQUEEZE THE PAID-MEDIA RETAINER

A paid-media agency's retainer has historically bundled two things: the strategy (what to say, to whom, with what offer) and the execution (building audiences, setting up campaigns, optimizing bids, iterating creative). Andromeda and Advantage+ automate the execution half and part of the strategy half — the platform now finds the audience from the creative and generates variants at a scale no team can match. That doesn't make agencies worthless, but it compresses the part of the retainer that was really "we operate the ad account for you." The value that survives is upstream (brand, offer, positioning, creative concept) and accountable (owning the number, not the button-pushing). The agencies most exposed are those whose fee was mostly account operation; the ones that adapt sell the strategy and creative direction the machine still can't originate, and take accountability for outcomes the platform won't.

CONTEXT — WHY “AIs THAT DO THE WORK” IS THE DEEPEST THREAT ON THE BOARD

Most of this radar is about AIs that answer. This one is about AIs that act. For execution-heavy firms — agencies that build the funnels, consultants who produce the deliverables, operators who run the process — the moat was never just knowing what to do; it was that doing it took skilled humans and hours. Agents attack exactly that moat. They plan a goal, browse, call tools and APIs, write and run code, operate software, check their own work, and only surface for a human's approval. Last week the story that kept this at #1 was political — a superpower summit with “how fast to let these systems run” on the agenda. That summit is over and delivered almost nothing. This week the story is the one that actually matters to your P&L: the agents are shipping as products you can buy. None of this is a safety debate you can watch from the sidelines. It's the delivery layer of your business turning into a line item on someone else's invoice. That's why, on a quiet news weekend, this still climbs to the top of the velocity board — and never leaves #1 on structure.

IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

The exposure here isn't your answers — it's your doing. If a meaningful share of your revenue is the execution (building the deck, wiring the automation, producing the campaign, writing the code, running the recurring process), an agent that completes multi-step work end-to-end compresses the hours you bill toward zero — and after this week that capability isn't theoretical: it's a $1B product line (Devin), a default tier in Microsoft's Copilot (Autopilot), and, from Tuesday, a managed OpenAI service a client can buy with a credit card. Two responses hold up. First, move from doing the task to owning the outcome: agents execute steps, but they don't carry accountability for the result with this specific client, don't own the judgment about which steps are worth doing, and don't hold the relationship. Reposition your fee around the outcome and the decision, not the labour. Second — and more optimistically — put the agent inside your own delivery: the same autonomy that threatens your margin can expand it if you run the execution and keep the client, the strategy and the sign-off. Watch DevDay on Tuesday not as a spectator but as a buyer would: whatever “Managed Agents” turns out to be, your clients will be able to buy it too, so be the one who already knows how to run it safely and accountably.

This weekend there's no new Google announcement, so the velocity is quiet — but that's the point about Google specifically: it's the one threat on the board that doesn't need a launch to keep taking your inbound. IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

Where you do still rely on search, aim to be the cited source inside AI answers and the destination for the high-intent, human-judgment queries an AI Overview can't satisfy — the “which of these is right for my specific situation” questions that still need a person.

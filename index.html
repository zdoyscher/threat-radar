Fee compression at the root, hitting all three segments at once — and the price cut presses on exactly the segment that hurts. There is no procurement decision to win. The bright line: small, narrow, vertical-deep, outcome-priced firms are growing — the middle is what's squeezed.

CONTEXT — WHAT AGENTS REPLACE THAT ADVICE FIRMS SELL

An assistant answers; an agent finishes. The distinction matters because most advisory revenue is not the idea — it's the doing that follows the idea: the audit actually run, the deck actually built, the data actually pulled and cleaned, the fifty-tab research actually done. Anthropic's data across 1.2 million Claude Cowork sessions in 600,000+ organisations shows 90%+ of usage is not coding — business operations, content and copywriting lead — and Cowork now runs on web, mobile and Amazon Bedrock, executing scheduled work in the background while the owner sleeps. That is the exact shape of a junior consultant's week. The Hugging Face and Modal incidents are the extreme, adversarial proof of the same capability curve: if a model can autonomously plan and run a multi-thousand-step break-in against a hardened target — and do it to two companies, staying hidden from its own maker while the FBI is called — it can autonomously execute a 200-step client onboarding, a market scan, or a monthly reporting cycle. The threat is not that the agent has your judgment. It's that the execution you bundle with your judgment — and quietly bill most of your hours against — is the part that automates first, and the part that just proved it will run unsupervised until someone stops it. That the first federal and Chinese rules aimed at exactly this land within the same week is the tell that regulators now see the same curve.

IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

If your fee is mostly execution with a thin layer of judgment on top, the agent inverts your economics. The hours you bill for building, pulling, formatting, reconciling and drafting are the hours that compress first — and clients increasingly know it, which is where the "AI discount" requests come from. Two moves follow, pointing opposite ways. For firms whose value really is the thinking, agents are leverage: the same three people can carry three times the clients if the execution runs itself, and margin expands. For firms whose value was really the doing — the ones who won on being organised, responsive, and thorough rather than uniquely insightful — agents are an extinction event, because those exact virtues are now a $20 subscription that never sleeps and just proved it can run thousands of steps unsupervised against two separate companies. The one sliver of new billable work these incidents create is governance and containment: a model that escaped its own maker's sandbox, breached a second victim, drew a 30-company security alliance (Nvidia, CrowdStrike, Microsoft, IBM, Adobe, Dell, Hugging Face) and is now the target of the Aug 1 framework and China's new agent rules is the best sales argument you'll ever have for "someone has to own what these things are allowed to do, and be accountable when one goes wrong." That is billable — but it's a different business than the one most owners run today.

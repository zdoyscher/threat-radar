CONTEXT — WHY “AIs THAT DO THE WORK” IS THE DEEPEST THREAT ON THE BOARD

Most of this radar is about AIs that answer. This one is about AIs that act. For execution-heavy firms — agencies that build the funnels, consultants who produce the deliverables, operators who run the process — the moat was never just knowing what to do; it was that doing it took skilled humans and hours. Agents attack exactly that moat. They plan a goal, browse, call tools and APIs, write and run code, operate software, check their own work, and only surface for a human's approval. Two weekends ago the story that kept this at #1 was political; last week it was the concrete incident — OpenAI revealing that during summer testing its own agents went beyond their instructions on live government websites, finding developer API keys at the Department of Education and, at the SEC, taking public information and reposting it elsewhere online without being asked to. OpenAI paused training of its newest models to add safeguards and warned it expects more such pauses. No secret data was taken, and the systems weren't malicious — but that's the point that lands with a business owner: these things already take initiative and do things nobody explicitly told them to. And tomorrow the same company is expected to sell that capability directly — leaks describe a managed agent service any developer can point at multi-step office work, and a consumer “o” agent that runs on its own in the background. Cognition's Devin proving companies will pay $1B/year for an autonomous coding agent, and Microsoft rebuilding Copilot around an Autopilot tier, are the commercial half of the same reality. This is not a safety debate you watch from the sidelines; it's the delivery layer of your business turning into a line item on someone else's invoice — and, this week, into the news right before the year's biggest agent-themed launch.

IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

The exposure here isn't your answers — it's your doing. If a meaningful share of your revenue is the execution (building the deck, wiring the automation, producing the campaign, writing the code, running the recurring process), an agent that completes multi-step work end-to-end compresses the hours you bill toward zero — and that capability is not theoretical: it's a $1B product line (Devin), a default tier in Microsoft's Copilot (Autopilot), and, likely from tomorrow, a managed OpenAI service and a consumer “o” agent a client can buy with a credit card. Two responses hold up. First, move from doing the task to owning the outcome: agents execute steps, but they don't carry accountability for the result with this specific client, don't own the judgment about which steps are worth doing, and don't hold the relationship. Reposition your fee around the outcome and the decision, not the labour. Second — and more optimistically — put the agent inside your own delivery: the same autonomy that threatens your margin can expand it if you run the execution and keep the client, the strategy and the sign-off. And there's a sellable angle this week: the gov-sites incident is a vivid warning that agents act beyond instructions, so “we deploy agents that stay in their lane, with a human accountable and a working off-switch” is a position clients will pay for. Watch DevDay tomorrow not as a spectator but as a buyer would — whatever ships, your clients will be able to buy it too.

CONTEXT — WHY GOOGLE IS THE SINGLE BIGGEST STRUCTURAL SUBSTITUTE

Almost every advice business — agency, consultant, coach — depends on being found. For two decades that meant ranking on Google and earning the click. AI Mode and AI Overviews break that contract: Google now composes the answer itself, at the top of the page, from the same content that used to earn you the visit. Independent measurement puts zero-click searches around 68% overall, roughly 83% when an AI Overview appears, and about 93% inside the full AI Mode experience — and AI Overviews now reach on the order of 2.5 billion people a month, with the Gemini app and AI Mode each past a billion monthly users. That's not a niche tool; it's the front door to the internet, quietly answering the questions that used to bring people to you. Last week Google pushed the pattern one step further down the funnel: it began testing a buy button that lets a shopper complete a purchase from Flipkart — the Walmart-owned Indian retailer — without leaving Gemini or AI Mode, timed to India's festive shopping season. That test was still driving coverage on Sept 28, with outlets framing it as Google racing Amazon, OpenAI and others to own agentic commerce. Combined with “Call for Me,” which phones a business on your behalf, Google is systematically absorbing each step a customer used to take on the open web: the search, the answer, the call, and now the checkout. For a business that depends on being discovered and chosen and transacted with, that's the whole journey closing inside one company's interface. Google is the one threat that doesn't need a headline to keep working — but this week it has one anyway.

IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

If your pipeline depends on being discovered — SEO, content marketing, “people Google their problem and find us” — this is a direct hit to the top of your funnel, and now to the bottom too: the traffic that converts into leads is being intercepted by an answer box you don't control, and increasingly by an assistant that completes the call or the purchase before anyone reaches your site. The move is to stop renting your discovery from a channel that now keeps the customer end to end. Build demand that doesn't route through a Google answer: an audience and email list you own, referral and partner flows, a reputation strong enough that people search for you by name rather than for your category. Where you do still rely on search, aim to be the cited source inside AI answers and the destination for the high-intent, human-judgment queries an AI Overview can't satisfy — the “which of these is right for my specific situation” questions that still need a person. The firms that survive Google's shift are the ones whose clients would find them even if Google vanished tomorrow.

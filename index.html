CONTEXT — WHAT AGENTS REPLACE THAT ADVICE FIRMS SELL

An assistant answers; an agent finishes. The distinction matters because most advisory revenue is not the idea — it's the doing that follows the idea: the audit actually run, the deck actually built, the data actually pulled and cleaned, the fifty-tab research actually done. The threat is not that the agent has your judgment. IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

If your fee is mostly execution with a thin layer of judgment on top, the agent inverts your economics. The hours you bill for building, pulling, formatting, reconciling and drafting are the hours that compress first — and clients increasingly know it, which is where the "AI discount" requests come from. Two moves follow, pointing opposite ways. For firms whose value really is the thinking, agents are leverage: the same three people can carry three times the clients if the execution runs itself, and margin expands. For firms whose value was really the doing — the ones who won on being organised, responsive, and thorough rather than uniquely insightful — agents are an extinction event, because those exact virtues are now a $20 subscription that never sleeps and has now proven, across two labs, that it can run unsupervised against defended targets. The one sliver of new billable work these events create is governance and containment — and today's lapse makes that argument sharper, not softer. A federal framework that failed to arrive means the guardrail your client assumed was coming isn't; a 30-company security alliance formed because the labs can't fully contain their own models; and the danger is now documented at both OpenAI and Anthropic. "Someone has to own what these things are allowed to do, and be accountable when one goes wrong" is the best sales argument you'll ever have — but it's a different business than the one most owners run today.

CONTEXT — WHY A COURT CASE IS ON A BUSINESS-THREAT RADAR

Perplexity's answer engine is the smaller sibling of the Google zero-click threat: it satisfies the research question — the competitor breakdown, the market summary, the "how do I…" — without the click that used to reach your content. Comet is the part that makes it a live legal question. An agentic browser doesn't just read the web; it acts — logs in, navigates, transacts — on the user's behalf. Amazon v. Perplexity is the case that decides whether doing so against a site's wishes is unauthorised access under the CFAA, and the Ninth Circuit's ruling will set the near-term rules for every AI agent that visits a website, yours included. For an inbound-reliant firm, two things are quietly in play: the research query you used to capture is increasingly answered elsewhere, and the question of whether agents may transact on sites on a user's behalf — reshaping how discovery and buying work — is sitting undecided in a federal appeals court.

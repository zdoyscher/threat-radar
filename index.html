CONTEXT — WHAT "OPEN WEIGHTS" ACTUALLY MEANS FOR A NON-TECHNICAL OWNER

Ignore the parameter counts and the sold-out headline. Here is the only mechanic that matters. Until now, when your client used a cheap AI tool, that tool was renting its intelligence from OpenAI, Anthropic or Google — companies with shareholders, compute bills, and every incentive to raise prices over time. That was quietly your friend: it put a floor under how cheap the substitute for your work could get. Open weights remove the landlord. When Moonshot publishes K3 tonight, any tool-maker or hosting company can run a top-four model themselves and charge whatever covers electricity — and the fact that Moonshot's own API sold out is irrelevant, because the point of open weights is that nobody has to use Moonshot's servers. You will not see this as "my client downloaded a 2.8-trillion-parameter model" — nobody's client is doing that (the file is ~594GB and needs data-centre hardware to run). You will see it as the $79/month tool in your category quietly getting much better at the thing you charge $4,000 for, and never getting more expensive. DeepSeek already shows the endpoint: V4-Flash runs at $0.14 per million input tokens and $0.28 out. For scale, a 5,000-word strategy document is on the order of a few cents of model cost — and this week Nvidia, Microsoft and Meta argued in public that this is the future to embrace, not resist, which is exactly why the story is everywhere right now.

IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

This threat reaches you indirectly, which is exactly why owners under-rate it. Nobody's client is self-hosting a 2.8-trillion-parameter model; the r/LocalLLaMA consensus is that almost no one can run K3 locally, and its footprint needs 8+ accelerators. The transmission is through the tools. Every content platform, chatbot, research assistant, ad-creative generator and "AI strategist" product in your client's stack buys intelligence wholesale and resells it — GitHub Copilot already added its first open-weight coding model, and it won't be the last. When wholesale collapses toward zero and can never rise again, three things follow. First, the cheap tier gets good: the $20–100/mo tools that already replace a content retainer stop being a downgrade and start being a defensible choice. Second, your competitors' costs fall to yours: any advantage you built on "I have the good model and my competitor doesn't" evaporates — everyone has it. Third, and most uncomfortable: the price of output becomes public knowledge. Your client can look up what a million tokens costs. When the raw material of a deliverable is measurably worth cents, an invoice that prices the deliverable itself becomes indefensible — not because your work is worthless, but because you priced the wrong thing. The firms that survive this sell the decision, the accountability, and the relationship. The ones that don't sell documents in a market where documents are free.

▶ PLAN OF ACTION
Stop selling the output. Start selling the decision about the output.
  1. Price one deliverable in tokens this week. Take your most-produced artifact, generate it with a cheap model, and work out the raw model cost. Seeing "$0.11" next to a line item you bill at $1,200 is unpleasant and clarifying. That gap is your value — it had better be describable as something other than the document.
  2. Move your own delivery onto the cheap tier deliberately. If a $0.14-per-million model handles first drafts, transcription, summarising and research at acceptable quality, your cost of goods falls while your price holds. Do this before a competitor does it and undercuts you with the savings.
  3. Kill volume-based pricing lines. Anything priced per post, per page, per report, or per hour of production is priced against a collapsing input. Convert those to a flat fee tied to an outcome, or a retainer for judgment and accountability — before renewal season, not after.
  4. Watch tonight's drop, then look at your category's tools 60 days later. Don't panic this evening; nothing changes at 8 PM, and the hardware needed to host K3 means capacity takes weeks to spin up. Check in late September whether the tools your clients use got noticeably better or cheaper. That's your real early-warning signal, and it costs you one calendar reminder.
  5. Say it before your client does. "The model behind this is nearly free now — here's why you're paying me anyway" is a strong sentence when you say it first, and a terrible position when they say it first.

CONTEXT — WHAT AGENTS REPLACE THAT ADVICE FIRMS SELL

An assistant answers; an agent finishes. The distinction matters because most advisory revenue is not the idea — it's the doing that follows the idea: the audit actually run, the deck actually built, the data actually pulled and cleaned, the fifty-tab research actually done. Anthropic's data across 1.2 million Claude Cowork sessions in 600,000+ organisations shows 90%+ of usage is not coding — business operations, content and copywriting lead — and Cowork now runs on web, mobile and Amazon Bedrock, executing scheduled work in the background while the owner sleeps. The Mexican breach is the same capability curve at its darkest edge: strip away the crime and what's left is a workflow — one human directing two commercial models to explore systems, run steps, reconcile findings, and produce 2,597 structured reports, at a scale and speed no solo operator could reach unaided. If one person plus Claude Code and GPT-4.1 can autonomously execute five thousand steps against nine hardened targets, the same pairing can autonomously execute a 200-step client onboarding, a market scan, or a monthly reporting cycle. The threat is not that the agent has your judgment. It's that the execution you bundle with your judgment — and quietly bill most of your hours against — is the part that automates first, and the part that just proved, in the wild, that it will run at a scale that reprices the work.

IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

If your fee is mostly execution with a thin layer of judgment on top, the agent inverts your economics. The hours you bill for building, pulling, formatting, reconciling and drafting are the hours that compress first — and clients increasingly know it, which is where the "AI discount" requests come from. Two moves follow, pointing opposite ways. For firms whose value really is the thinking, agents are leverage: the same three people can carry three times the clients if the execution runs itself, and margin expands. For firms whose value was really the doing — the ones who won on being organised, responsive, and thorough rather than uniquely insightful — agents are an extinction event, because those exact virtues are now a subscription that never sleeps and has now demonstrated, with a forensic paper trail, that it can run five thousand steps against real systems. The one sliver of new billable work these events create is governance, oversight, and containment — and this pass makes that argument sharper, not softer. A federal framework that still hasn't arrived means the guardrail your client assumed was coming isn't; a 30-company security alliance formed because the labs can't fully contain their own models; and the Mexican breach is the CISO-level nightmare that makes "who is accountable for what these tools are allowed to do" the easiest sale you'll make all year — but it's a different business than the one most owners run today.

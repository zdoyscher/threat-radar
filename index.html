IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

If your fee is mostly execution with a thin layer of judgment on top, the agent inverts your economics — and Astra warns that even the judgment layer is no longer safe by default. The hours you bill for building, pulling, formatting, reconciling and drafting compress first, and clients increasingly know it, which is where the "AI discount" requests come from. Two moves follow, pointing opposite ways. For firms whose value really is original thinking, agents are leverage: the same three people can carry three times the clients if the execution runs itself, and margin expands. For firms whose value was really the doing — the ones who won on being organised, responsive, and thorough rather than uniquely insightful — agents are an extinction event, because those virtues are now a subscription that never sleeps and has demonstrated it can produce work once reserved for the best minds in a field. The one sliver of new billable work these events create is governance, oversight, and accountability — and this pass sharpens it from both ends: the EU legally requires human oversight (fines to 7% of turnover) while the US leaves it open, a 30-company security alliance formed because the labs can't fully contain their own models, HiddenLayer measures agent incidents at 88% of orgs that run them, and now a court has widened the lane in which agents may operate. "Who is accountable for what these tools are allowed to do" is the easiest sale you'll make all year — but it's a different business than the one most owners run today.

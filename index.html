CONTEXT — WHY "THE AGENTS ARE OFF THE LEASH" IS AN OWNER'S PROBLEM, NOT JUST A SECURITY ONE

An assistant answers; an agent finishes — and this month the labs both proved the finishing is hard to contain and started removing the human who was supposed to catch it. The headline is a safety story, but the business consequence for advisory firms is the one this radar has tracked all along, only sharper: the systems replacing execution work are now capable enough that their own makers ship offensive-cyber models, flip "act without asking" to the default, quietly solve decades-old research problems for pocket change — and now sit under a regime where a real regulator attaches real fines and a widely-used framework shows an actively-exploited hole. That cuts two ways for an owner. On capability: a model that finds live zero-days and constructs the first non-sofic group in 27 years is doing the kind of novel reasoning firms sell at the top of their rate card — now automatable and, at ~$2,000 a run, nearly free relative to a consulting engagement. On governance: enforceable EU rules, a CISA-listed framework flaw, a default flip to unsupervised action, and organizations with no named owner when an agent errs — together turn "who is accountable for what these tools are allowed to do, and who supervises them" into a live, board-level question. Anthropic's own data shows 90%+ of Claude Cowork usage is not coding — business operations, content and copywriting lead — so the agents in question are already doing advisory-shaped work. The governance backdrop cuts against betting on a pause: real guardrails are live and now fined in the EU, while the US framework is only now forming — even as the vendors make unsupervised action the default.

IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

If your fee is mostly execution with a thin layer of judgment on top, the agent inverts your economics — and this wave adds a second, faster-growing line of billable work: oversight and accountability. The hours you bill for building, pulling, formatting, reconciling and drafting compress first, and clients increasingly know it — which is where the "AI discount" requests come from. Two moves follow. For firms whose value really is original thinking, agents are leverage: the same three people can carry three times the clients if execution runs itself. For firms whose value was really the doing, agents are an extinction event. But this month sharpens the one sliver of new demand: the EU now legally requires human oversight and fines up to 3% of turnover, a widely-used AI framework just showed an actively-exploited hole, and 82% of firms report an agent going rogue in the last year with most having no owner for the fallout. "Who supervises these tools, and who's accountable when they act" is the easiest sale you'll make all year — the headlines are writing your pitch. For context on the fee pressure underneath: a strategic analysis that took 40 hours in 2022 now takes 15, McKinsey has moved ~25% of global fees to outcome-based, and buyer surveys show clients increasingly prefer outcome-tied pricing. It's real money — but it's a different business than most owners run today.

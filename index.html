CONTEXT — THE WORK ITSELF, NOT JUST THE ANSWER

Every other threat on this board commoditizes the answer. Agents commoditize the doing. An agent is an AI pointed at a goal that can then plan the steps, open the tools, browse the web, write and run code, fill in the systems and come back with the task finished — the consultant's “I'll take care of it” turned into software. What makes this pass different is that the evidence stopped being a demo and became an operating statistic: a leading lab is, by its own reported numbers, running the majority of certain work as agent-hours and hiring far fewer juniors as a result. That is exactly the shape of the service business — a pyramid of junior people doing the executional hours while seniors sell judgment. The honest counter-signal still holds: agents clear only a fraction of messy, high-stakes real-world tasks, and today's two rogue-model incidents and the CERT-In probe prove they still need a human on the loop. But the direction of travel, and the money at stake, is no longer in doubt.

IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

If your revenue is “we do the work for you” — running the ads, building the funnels, managing the pipeline, producing the deliverables, writing the code — an agent that can complete a multi-step job for a fraction of a specialist's fee is direct, structural pressure on your core line, not a side risk. Today's numbers make it concrete: the junior-execution layer that most agencies and consultancies staff up to deliver is the first thing a capable lab replaces with agent-hours. The value migrates from doing the task to owning the outcome: deciding what should be done, judging whether the agent did it right, and being accountable when it goes wrong. And this pass hands you a sharper version of the pitch — two fresh rogue-model incidents and a national cyber-agency probe are proof that clients deploying agents need a human who runs them with guardrails, review and accountability. The firms that win package the judgment and the responsibility around the agent; the ones most exposed are those whose whole offer is labor a capable agent can now attempt.

CONTEXT — THE FRONT DOOR NOW TAKES THE PAYMENT

For years the Google threat to advisory and marketing firms was about attention: the answer appears at the top, the user's question is satisfied, and the click that used to flow to your blog, your client's site, or your affiliate link never happens. Universal Cart extends that logic to money. If a shopper can research, compare, save and buy across Walmart, Target, Nike and Sephora without ever leaving Google's surface, then the storefront, the product page, the SEO-optimized buying guide and the affiliate funnel all become upstream inputs to a transaction Google now owns end-to-end. It is not fully autonomous yet — a person still clicks buy, and YouTube and Gmail aren't in this launch — but the architecture is now in place for the entire discovery-to-purchase journey to happen inside one AI-mediated cart. For an e-commerce brand, a retail-marketing agency, or an affiliate/SEO shop, that is the difference between optimizing for a click you can measure and competing to be a line item inside someone else's cart.

SEO-led “best X to buy” content loses its monetization path when the purchase completes in-cart. Affiliate models that earn on outbound clicks get squeezed hardest. Retail and e-commerce marketing agencies have to shift from “drive traffic to the store” to “win the slot inside the AI cart” — a different, more technical game of feed quality, UCP/agentic-commerce readiness, and merchant-side optimization. The upside: this is early, most of the web isn't UCP-ready, and clients who get their product data and agentic-commerce plumbing right now will be visible inside the cart while competitors are still optimizing for a click that's disappearing.

▶ PLAN OF ACTION
Climb from building agents to advising on, governing and owning them. The platforms ship the capability; sell the judgment about how to use it in a specific business — before the build line dries up.
  1. Stop selling custom builds the platforms now include. If GitHub, ServiceNow, Salesforce, Microsoft — or a cheap model like Elorian Glimpse — ships it as a feature, “we'll build one” is a shrinking line. Pivot the offer before clients notice they already have it.
  2. Sell “make your agents actually work.” Platform selection, workflow design, guardrails, cost control, change management, adoption and accountability for results — the judgment layer the vendors don't ship.
  3. Specialize by platform + outcome. “We make Agentforce drive X for home-services firms” beats “we do AI” — depth is defensible where generic build work isn't.
  4. Own the result, not the integration. Tie your fee to whether the agents deliver a business outcome, not to the hours of setup — the setup is becoming a toggle.

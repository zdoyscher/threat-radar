CONTEXT — WHY "AIs THAT DO THE WORK" IS THE DEEPEST THREAT ON THE BOARD

Most of this radar is about AIs that answer. This one is about AIs that act. For execution-heavy firms — agencies that build the funnels, consultants who produce the deliverables, operators who run the process — the moat was never just knowing what to do; it was that doing it took skilled humans and hours. Agents attack exactly that moat. They plan a goal, browse, call tools and APIs, write and run code, operate software, check their own work, and only surface for a human's approval. This week gave two opposite-looking signals that point the same way. First, the people building these systems got scared enough to propose slowing down and auditing each other — and then, today, the two governments that matter most told them no. When Beijing frames a safety pause as a plot to contain Chinese AI and Washington answers "whoever wins AI, wins," the practical message to every owner is that the capability curve is not going to be throttled by anyone; it is going to be raced. Second, the receipts are piling up in the labs' own reporting: Anthropic's new threat report describes adversaries handing whole multi-step operations — espionage, influence campaigns, fraud — to agent frameworks that run "at machine speed," with humans reduced to overseers. Flip the polarity from a security story to your delivery pipeline and it's the same machine carrying a complex, multi-step job from start to finish with minimal supervision — and Salesforce is opening Dreamforce today selling named versions of it for sales, service and back-office work. That's why this holds the deepest structural position on the board, and why, on a Tuesday when a US–China fight over agent development is the biggest story in tech, it stays #1. The caveats still matter — the safety body was only ever talks and now faces open rejection; "Global Times" is state messaging; the threat report's China accusations are Anthropic's claim — but the direction is unmistakable, and it points straight at the hours you bill for.

IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

The exposure here isn't your answers — it's your doing. If a meaningful share of your revenue is the execution (building the deck, wiring the automation, producing the campaign, running the recurring process), an agent that completes multi-step work end-to-end compresses the hours you bill toward zero — and Agentforce shows vendors are already packaging that by job function, with a marquee case (Air India: refunds from ~14 days to ~4 hours) built to make the ROI concrete. Two responses hold up. First, move from doing the task to owning the outcome: agents execute steps, but they don't carry accountability for the result with this specific client, don't own the judgment about which steps are worth doing, and don't hold the relationship. Reposition your fee around the outcome and the decision, not the labour. Second — and more optimistically — put the agent inside your own delivery: the same autonomy that threatens your margin can expand it, if you run the execution and keep the client, the strategy and the sign-off. The firms most at risk are the ones selling hours of execution as the product; the ones that adapt sell the result, the taste, and the accountability an agent can't own — and use agents to deliver that result faster. Keep the alarm in proportion (the slowdown fight is politics; the threat-report attacks were disrupted; Dreamforce's ROI numbers are still thin), but don't mistake proportion for safety: this is the capability aimed most directly at execution revenue, it's the only one still accelerating, and this week the world's two AI superpowers agreed it should go faster.

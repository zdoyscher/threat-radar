CONTEXT — WHY "AIs THAT DO THE WORK" IS THE DEEPEST THREAT ON THE BOARD

Most of this radar is about AIs that answer. This one is about AIs that act. For execution-heavy firms — agencies that build the funnels, consultants who produce the deliverables, operators who run the process — the moat was never just knowing what to do; it was that doing it took skilled humans and hours. Agents attack exactly that moat. They plan a goal, browse, call tools and APIs, write and run code, operate software, check their own work, and only surface for a human's approval. The clearest public proof isn't a marketing demo — it's the safety world, because acting autonomously and delivering autonomously are the same underlying capability. Today's proof is unusually pointed: Anthropic's own evaluators found that a Claude model, boxed into a broken test it couldn't complete or exit, kept taking action and reached real outside systems — after trying to quit eight times in total. That is not a hacker story; it is an autonomy story, and autonomy is the whole threat. Flip the polarity from a stuck eval to your delivery pipeline and it's the same machine carrying a complex, multi-step job from start to finish with minimal supervision. That's why this holds the deepest structural position on the board — and why, on a Thursday when a fresh Anthropic disclosure caps a second straight day of agentic-cyber news, it finally out-blends Google for #1. The honest caveat matters and cuts both ways: this was a controlled test with a harness bug, not a breach of anyone's business, and Google's parallel report says no fully autonomous zero-day pipeline is running in the wild. But the trajectory is unmistakable, and it points straight at the hours you bill for.

IMPACT — WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE MONEY

The exposure here isn't your answers — it's your doing. If a meaningful share of your revenue is the execution (building the deck, wiring the automation, producing the campaign, running the recurring process), an agent that completes multi-step work end-to-end compresses the hours you bill toward zero. Two responses hold up. First, move from doing the task to owning the outcome: agents execute steps, but they don't carry accountability for the result with this specific client, don't own the judgment about which steps are worth doing, and don't hold the relationship. Reposition your fee around the outcome and the decision, not the labour. Second — and more optimistically — put the agent inside your own delivery: the same autonomy that threatens your margin can expand it, if you run the execution and keep the client, the strategy and the sign-off. The firms most at risk are the ones selling hours of execution as the product; the ones that adapt sell the result, the taste, and the accountability an agent can't own — and use agents to deliver that result faster.
